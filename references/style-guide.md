# Visual style guide — museum registration print (套版分版样张)

## Material feeling

The finished image must read as one physical object: a printmaker's proofing record — same-size impressions on warm off-white uncoated paper. Ink sits ON the paper — slightly uneven density, chalky edges, fine tooth, tiny bare-paper specks where ink skipped. Never a clean digital vector, never glossy, never a photo filter.

## Sheet layout

- Default: SIMPLE 4-STEP diagram — 4 rectangles of IDENTICAL size in a 2x2 grid, strict reading order left-to-right, top-to-bottom. Cell 4 IS the finished print (no separate finished-only cell). Larger grids (3x2/4x2 with a leading finished cell) only on explicit request.
- Orientation follows the rolled/chosen canvas orientation (square by default; portrait/landscape supported) — cells and sheet share the master's aspect.
- Order is marked by small bold numerals just OUTSIDE each cell's UPPER-left corner. No gutter arrows — the 2x2 reading order is self-evident.
- The print area is a plain RECTANGLE: sharp corners, NO border, NO frame, NO rounded corners, NO drawn outline. Its edge is simply where the printed color areas end on the paper.
- Thin hairline registration crosses (套准十字线) may sit just OUTSIDE two corners of each rectangle (or at the four sheet corners when composing with code); numerals and crosses are the only marks besides the main text strip — never inside the print area, never clipped by the sheet edge.

## Layer count and multi-color blocks

- Default 4 stamping steps (2x2 grid); up to 8 only on explicit request — accumulation reliability degrades quickly beyond 4.
- **Accumulative steps (hard rule)**: step cell N+1 = cell N's content PLUS exactly one new stamped layer. Earlier layers never disappear or change between steps — the sequence builds stamp by stamp, and the LAST cell is the finished print.
- **The final step is a big visible jump**: the anchor's PATTERN (e.g. bingata 纹样) is a separate DETAIL block stamped in the final step together with the keyline and text — its earlier cell shows only the plain base color. A final step that adds only text is a failure.
- A step may carry SEVERAL colors from one block (one pull, multi-color) — typical for the anchor garment's pattern block, bingata-style. Show all its colors in that single step, butting or knocked out, never mixed within the pull.
- Each layer is flat geometric shapes in its color family; no gradients, no photographic texture, no fine detail.

## Fidelity to the source

- The reduction stays CLOSE to the photo: anchor silhouette follows the actual garment/object outline; walls, trees, ground keep the photo's positions, heights and scale; the figure keeps the photo's proportions.
- Simplify texture, not geometry — sheet and photo side by side read as the same picture.

## The six layers

| Layer | Content | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Paper | quiet field: sky, walls, road, negative space | warm off-white with visible grain; carries at least 25% of the print interior |
| 2 Background color | the largest atmosphere plane (sky tint, distant field) | one flat color family, simplest shape |
| 3 Mid-ground | buildings, trees, terrain masses | clean angular or lobed planes; sparse dash details only |
| 4 Anchor color | the identification anchor's dominant planes | the most saturated layer; keeps the anchor's silhouette exact |
| 5 Accents | small repeated marks (flowers, lights, signs, pattern hints) | scattered with uneven spacing, never gridded |
| 6 Keyline | text strip, minimum joint/fold marks | near-black or darkest source color; NO frame, NO border, NO dashed silhouette outline around figures/objects (sticker cut-lines are forbidden); sparse — shapes do the talking |

- Layers 2-5 each use ONE color family (a family may shift slightly in density between shapes, like uneven inking — never a gradient).
- Wherever two color layers overlap, darken the overlap (overprint effect) rather than blending.

## Ink state (each plate pulled differently)

The plates were inked and pulled at different moments — never render all layers with identical uniform density:

- **Fresh/dense**: one layer gets the richest, most even coverage — usually the anchor layer, since it was printed last and with care.
- **Dry-roller**: one or two layers pulled with a starved roller — streaky, mottled, bare-paper specks showing through, edges broken. Suits large background or mid-ground planes.
- **Second-pull medium**: one layer printed with a slightly thicker, denser pull — visibly more opaque than the dry layers but not glossy.
- **Uneven pressure keyline**: the keyline is thin and pale in places, pooling darker in others; dash ends vary.

The same ink states must be visible in the matching plate cells, so the grid reads as a genuine proofing record.

## Overprint and shape edges (the defining trait)

The reference standard: real souvenir rubber-stamp prints. NOT watercolor, NOT opaque digital color.

- **Inks are slightly TRANSLUCENT.** Each layer is pulled over whatever is already printed, so every shape shows TWO clearly different tones: its PURE ink color over bare paper, and a DARKER MIXED tone wherever it crosses an earlier ink layer — multiply-like overprint. Yellow kimono over grey road → olive-tinted yellow; red blossom over green bush → deep brown-red; green foliage over pink blossom → muted plum-green. Uniform color across overlap and non-overlap zones of one shape is physically impossible for a real stamp and is a failure.
- **Hard clean shape edges everywhere.** No watercolor blooms, no feathered bleeding, no pigment-pool halos, no soft tonal edges, no gradients. The mixed overlap tone keeps crisp union shapes — never soft blending, never pastel averaging.
- **Stamp texture lives INSIDE the fills**: grainy, slightly streaky rubber-stamp ink, tiny bare-paper specks, uneven density — like real stamp pads, not like wet paint.
- **Knockout for side-by-side collisions**: where two shapes merely touch or crowd (not overlap), separate them with a thin white paper gap, as in real stamp separations; keep the choice consistent per color pair.
- **Reserved voids for big foreground blocks (authentic carving logic)**: a large occluding block (anchor garment/figure) is NOT overprinted onto the blocks beneath — earlier blocks are carved with a silhouette-shaped VOID (the blank person-shaped silhouette is already visible in the early step cells), and the foreground block stamps INTO the void as pure color on bare paper with hard butting edges. No mixing between the foreground block and what it occludes; a thin misregistration paper-sliver along the silhouette is welcome.
- **Keyline does the defining**: interior detail and shape identity come from the near-black linework and clean silhouette edges, never from tonal softness or shading.

## Registration (套版错位)

- In the FINISHED print, every layer is offset from the others by a small, visible amount (1-3 mm at final scale), each in its own consistent direction — like blocks that were carved and mounted by hand. The offsets differ between the dry and damp layers: the dry-roller layer drifts most, the fresh layer sits closest to true.
- The keyline is also misregistered: its dashes sit slightly off the color edges they describe.
- In each PLATE cell the shapes sit at their nominal position — the accumulated offsets only appear in the finished print.

## Text strip

- Inside the finished print near its bottom, and at the same position in the keyline plate cell: `[PLACE] · [YYYY.MM.DD]`, small capitals, generously letter-spaced, printed in the keyline layer.
- Letterforms in vintage postal-cancellation style (邮戳): slightly condensed serif or stamp-engraved shapes, unevenly inked with light patchy fade like a partially worn rubber postmark, occasional tiny ink gaps inside strokes, baseline very slightly wobbling. Must remain perfectly legible and misspelling-free — "worn postmark", never distressed grunge, never cracked or splattered.
- If the place name is uncertain, print the date alone. Never invent a plausible-sounding place.

## Figures

- From behind: hair-color shape + accessories in true colors. No face.
- Facing camera: light neutral face tone, two short dashes for eyes, one stroke for a mouth, loosely inked. Never beautified, never blank, never black-filled.
- Clothing: true source colors, reduced to 2-3 flat planes per garment.
- Distant people: compact anonymous silhouettes.

## Color

- 5-6 families total (paper + 4 color layers + keyline), derived from the source photo.
- Museum-print palette: slightly dusted, print-ink versions of the source colors — saturated but not neon, light but not pastel-washed.
- One small accent may be punchier than the rest (e.g. a vermilion detail on a muted scheme).

## Avoid

- Frames, borders, rounded-corner seals, black outlines around the print — real registration proofs have no drawn frame.
- Gradients, airbrush softness, glossy 3D, anime, watercolor in ANY form (blooms, bleeds, washes, granulation), soft blended overlaps, pastel averaging where colors meet, photographic texture.
- Detailed windows, brick courses, leaf veins, fabric folds, anatomy — structure via shape edges and sparse dashes only.
- Extra text, logos, serial numbers, museum names, decorative pseudo-writing; distressed or illegible lettering.
- Perfect alignment in the finished print (kills the block-print feel) and watermarks.
